# Copy baseline extraction

Use when a real prospect already has meaningful first-party website copy.

The goal is not to preserve every sentence. It is to understand what the business is trying to communicate before rewriting it.

## Inspect

Prioritize:
- homepage
- About / Story / Philosophy
- primary service/product pages
- destination/experience pages
- first-party brochures or public PDFs when directly relevant

Do not crawl the entire site.

## Output

Create:

`research/COPY_BASELINE.md`

Required sections:

### Voice
Describe:
- formality
- warmth
- confidence
- technicality
- playfulness/restraint
- recurring rhetorical style

### Recurring vocabulary
Capture words and phrases that appear intentionally and repeatedly.

Separate:
- domain-native language
- brand-owned language
- generic marketing language that can be dropped

### Narrative pillars
What stories/themes does the current site intentionally invest in?

Examples:
- founder/origin
- place/geography
- craft/process
- hospitality
- heritage
- innovation when genuinely central
- community
- materiality
- philosophy

### Existing stories worth preserving
List specific stories, concepts, rituals, people, places, processes, or historical details that carry identity.

### Commercial messages
What does the site want visitors to understand or do?

### Preserve
Messaging/language that should survive the redesign in substance.

### Can simplify
Repetition, page-builder clutter, redundant operational text, generic filler, or weak hierarchy that can be improved.

### Unknown
Voice/story questions that remain unresolved.

## Rule

For institutional sites:

Improve hierarchy, not erase narrative.

Do not compress the site into only:
- value proposition
- benefits
- CTA
- objection handling

unless the site strategy explicitly classifies the project as campaign.

Existing first-party language is evidence of brand intention.

Do not blindly copy legacy prose; preserve meaning, voice, and narrative jobs while improving structure and clarity.
