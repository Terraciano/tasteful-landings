# Portfolio Design Principles

## Objective

Create premium websites for businesses selling expensive products or services.

The websites should feel credible, restrained, specific and intentional.

They should not look like startup templates.

## Niche over tech

Premium and luxury businesses should not default to a technology-company aesthetic.

The interface may be technically modern while remaining visually non-tech.

Prefer making the business feel:
- specialized
- established
- discreet
- domain-native
- culturally specific
- confident without over-explaining itself

Modernize execution, not personality.

Avoid defaulting to:
- SaaS layouts
- startup vocabulary
- gradient-driven identity
- excessive rounded UI
- glassmorphism
- app-like navigation
- dashboard aesthetics
- artificial futurism
- "innovation" as the visual theme

Prefer:
- editorial composition
- strong photography
- physical materials
- typography with character
- restrained interaction
- domain-specific details
- provenance
- craftsmanship
- expertise
- cultural references native to the industry

## Insider credibility

The site should feel designed by someone who understands the industry, not by someone applying a generic luxury template.

Prefer signals recognizable to informed buyers:
- specialist terminology
- product details
- provenance
- process
- materials
- specifications
- geography
- history when relevant
- domain-specific expertise

Avoid generic luxury language such as:
- elevate
- unparalleled
- redefine
- exceptional experiences
- where luxury meets innovation

## Identity preservation

Do not assume an established business wants its identity "modernized" into something else.

When relevant, preserve and amplify:
- origin
- founder story
- generational history
- longstanding reputation
- local context
- craft
- specialist culture
- referral-driven relationships

These are optional brand assets, not requirements for every business.

## Visual hierarchy

Every section should have one dominant idea.

Avoid layouts where every element has equal visual weight.

Prefer:
- strong photography
- asymmetry
- editorial layouts
- large whitespace
- disciplined typography
- deliberate density

## Typography

Typography should carry much of the visual identity.

Avoid using decorative typography simply to imply luxury.

Do not default to:
- Playfair Display
- Cormorant
- generic serif + gold combinations

A serif is valid when the actual brand/category direction justifies it.

## Color

Color palettes should come from the industry and client context.

Use restrained palettes.

Accent colors should have a reason to exist.

## Motion

Motion should reinforce hierarchy.

Avoid:
- animation on every element
- scroll effects without purpose
- excessive parallax
- motion that delays content

## Components

Do not default to reusable SaaS components visually.

Do not over-componentize technically.

Composition should remain editorial and domain-specific.
