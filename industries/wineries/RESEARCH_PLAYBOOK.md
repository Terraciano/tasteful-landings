# Wineries / Wine Estates — Research Playbook

Use after reading the client brief and `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`.

Do not begin with generic "luxury winery website" searches.

## 1. Resolve the winery subcategory

Classify the business into one primary commercial model:

- estate winery
- boutique winery
- premium wine brand
- wine tourism / tastings
- wine lodge / hospitality
- winery restaurant
- wine club / allocation
- export-focused producer
- negociant / producer brand
- multi-estate group
- vineyard real estate / branded estate

If multiple apply, identify the primary conversion goal first.

## 2. Resolve geography

For Mendoza, resolve the most specific useful area possible.

Examples:
- Mendoza
- Luján de Cuyo
- Maipú
- Valle de Uco
- Tupungato
- Tunuyán
- San Carlos
- Gualtallary
- Paraje Altamira
- Agrelo
- Las Compuertas

Geography affects:
- visual language
- tourism search intent
- terroir storytelling
- landscape
- hospitality
- category competitors

## 3. Category-language searches

Run relevant combinations:

- `winery <geography>`
- `bodega <geography>`
- `wine tasting <geography>`
- `wine tourism <geography>`
- `winery restaurant <geography>`
- `vineyard lodge <geography>`
- `Malbec <geography> winery`
- `premium wine producer <geography>`
- `wine export Argentina producer`

For Spanish-language local businesses also inspect:
- `bodega <geography>`
- `visita bodega <geography>`
- `degustación vinos <geography>`
- `turismo enológico <geography>`

Use the language the market actually uses.

## 4. Direct competitors

Find 3-5 businesses matching:
- same geography or adjacent zone
- similar scale
- similar price/positioning
- similar tourism/export emphasis
- similar buyer

Capture:
- homepage argument
- product structure
- estate story
- visit/booking flow
- trade/export path
- visual language
- trust signals
- CTA pattern
- what feels generic

## 5. Category leaders

Choose only relevant leaders.

Mendoza / Argentina examples to consider:
- Catena Zapata
- Zuccardi Valle de Uco
- Susana Balbo
- Salentein
- El Enemigo
- Achaval-Ferrer
- Lagarde
- Durigutti
- Bodega Norton
- Trapiche

Do not inspect all of them by default.

Use 2-4 whose commercial model and positioning are relevant.

Extract:
- product storytelling
- estate geography
- architecture
- label/wine hierarchy
- technical detail
- tourism/hospitality
- photography rhythm
- typography behavior

## 6. Boutique / niche searches

Run 1-4 relevant queries:

- `boutique winery <geography>`
- `small production winery <geography>`
- `family winery <geography>`
- `single vineyard winery <geography>`
- `estate wine <geography>`
- `winemaker project <geography>`
- `private tasting winery <geography>`
- `wine lodge <geography>`
- `vineyard restaurant <geography>`

Family ownership is a possible signal, not a requirement.

Look for:
- specificity
- small-production language
- parcel detail
- founder/winemaker authorship
- architectural identity
- reservation-led hospitality
- export/trade sophistication
- strong relationship to place

## 7. Adjacent references

Use only to solve a concrete design problem.

Good adjacent categories:
- boutique hospitality
- architecture studios
- olive estates
- fine dining
- watch/jewelry editorial product presentation
- book/magazine editorial layouts
- luxury rural hospitality

Examples of valid reasons:
- winery site lacks a strong way to present architecture
- hospitality flow needs better booking hierarchy
- wine collection needs better editorial product rhythm
- terroir story needs map/cartographic treatment

Limit: 0-2.

## 8. Insider signals to capture

Depending on the client:
- varietal
- blend
- vintage
- vineyard
- parcel
- altitude
- soil
- appellation / IG / GI / DOC
- harvest date
- yield
- fermentation
- vessel
- oak regime
- aging
- alcohol
- acidity / pH
- production quantity
- bottling
- winemaker
- viticulture
- irrigation
- organic / biodynamic practices
- importer/distributor
- critic scores
- restaurant/hospitality connection

Do not use technical detail merely to look sophisticated.

## 9. Hospitality signals

If visits matter, research:
- reservation flow
- tasting formats
- private vs group
- lunch/dining
- opening days/hours
- transfer/location context
- language options
- cancellation/deposit model
- accessibility
- event/private booking
- hotel/lodge integration

Do not assume ecommerce is the primary conversion if bookings are more valuable.

## 10. Visual questions

Research must answer:

- Is the estate itself the hero, or the wine?
- Is the buyer a tourist, collector, trade buyer, or all three?
- How central is Mendoza/Andes geography?
- Does architecture matter commercially?
- How technical should wine detail become?
- Is the brand historic, contemporary, experimental, or hospitality-led?
- What makes the brand feel specific rather than "generic premium wine"?
- Is product purchase, tasting reservation, or trade inquiry the primary CTA?
- Does the label system already carry a strong visual identity?
- Which materials/colors genuinely belong to the estate?

## 11. Anti-patterns

Explicitly flag:
- generic Tuscany imagery for Argentina
- beige-serif wine-template aesthetic
- fake heritage
- black/gold luxury
- vineyard stock unrelated to the estate
- endless tasting-note poetry with no useful facts
- SaaS cards/buttons
- excessive animations over landscape photography
- wine ecommerce patterns dominating a hospitality-led estate
- copy such as "where tradition meets innovation"
- romanticizing "family" when it is irrelevant to the brand

## 12. Research stop condition

In addition to the global stop condition, winery research is ready when:

- the commercial model is clear
- geography is specific enough
- primary conversion is clear
- product vs estate vs hospitality emphasis is resolved
- expected trust/provenance signals are known
- the agent can explain what makes this winery visually/category-specific without relying on "luxury", "premium", "rustic", or "elegant"
