# Private Aviation — Research Playbook

Use after reading the client brief and the general `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`.

Do not search for generic "luxury website inspiration" first.

## 1. Resolve the aviation subcategory

Classify the business into one primary category:

- aircraft brokerage / sales
- aircraft acquisition advisory
- charter brokerage
- aircraft management
- fixed-base operator (FBO)
- maintenance / MRO
- flight training
- aircraft manufacturer / OEM
- fractional ownership / membership
- specialist aviation service

If the client spans multiple categories, identify the primary revenue/conversion motion first.

## 2. Direct-competitor searches

For brokerage / acquisition:

- `private aircraft broker <geography>`
- `business jet sales <geography>`
- `aircraft acquisition advisory <geography>`
- `Gulfstream Bombardier broker <geography>`

For charter:

- `private jet charter <geography>`
- `private aviation charter broker <geography>`
- `business jet charter <geography>`

For management:

- `aircraft management <geography>`
- `private aircraft management <geography>`

Adapt the nouns to the client.

## 3. Category leaders to inspect

Choose relevant leaders, not all of them.

Brokerage / market:
- Jetcraft
- AvBuyer
- GlobalAir
- Controller

OEM / product storytelling:
- Gulfstream
- Bombardier Business Aircraft
- Dassault Aviation
- Embraer Executive Jets

Service / private-flight positioning:
- NetJets
- Flexjet
- VistaJet

These are pattern sources, not visual authorities by default.

## 4. Boutique / niche searches

Run 1-3 of:

- `boutique aircraft brokerage`
- `independent private jet broker`
- `aircraft broker family office`
- `principal led aircraft brokerage`
- `private aircraft advisory`
- `business aviation advisory firm`

Look for:
- discretion
- principal access
- specialist aircraft knowledge
- transaction expertise
- relationship language
- low-volume/high-touch positioning

Do not require family ownership.

## 5. Insider signals to capture

Pay attention to:
- manufacturer/model naming
- year/configuration
- serial number when industry-appropriate
- total time / cycles
- range
- passenger configuration
- maintenance/program status
- avionics
- location
- acquisition/sale process
- off-market access
- pre-buy inspection
- escrow/closing
- export/import
- regulatory/logistical expertise

Use only facts appropriate to the client.

## 6. Visual questions

Research must answer:

- Does this subcategory sell the aircraft or the advisor?
- How prominent should specifications be?
- Is inventory central or secondary?
- Is photography product-first, operational, or lifestyle?
- What does discretion look like without becoming visually empty?
- Which trust signals are expected by an informed buyer?
- What makes the site feel like aviation rather than generic luxury?

## 7. Anti-patterns

Explicitly flag:
- nightclub private-jet aesthetic
- champagne/model stock photography
- black-and-gold by default
- "VIP lifestyle" language
- app/dashboard treatment
- Silicon Valley SaaS composition
- generic innovation claims
- overuse of glass/gradients
