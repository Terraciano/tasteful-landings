# Private Aviation — Industry Brief

## Commercial context

Primary use cases:
- aircraft brokerage
- aircraft acquisition advisory
- private aircraft sales
- charter brokerage when explicitly requested

Typical purchase decisions are high-consideration, trust-heavy, and information-dense.

The page should not sell "luxury" in the abstract. It should communicate discreet competence around a valuable technical asset.

## Audience

Potential visitors include:
- aircraft owners
- high-net-worth individuals
- family offices
- corporate flight departments
- charter operators
- advisors acting on behalf of buyers or sellers

## Primary conversion behavior

Prefer high-intent actions:
- Request aircraft details
- Speak with an advisor
- Discuss an acquisition
- Arrange a private viewing
- List an aircraft

Avoid generic startup CTAs such as:
- Get started
- Join now
- Unlock more
- Start free

## Brand characteristics

Communicate:
- precision
- discretion
- technical fluency
- confidence
- global reach
- calm responsiveness

Do not communicate:
- nightclub luxury
- influencer wealth
- ostentation
- crypto-bro wealth signaling
- "VIP lifestyle" clichés

## Design dials

Suggested starting point:
- DESIGN_VARIANCE: 6
- MOTION_INTENSITY: 4
- VISUAL_DENSITY: 5

These are starting values, not immutable tokens.

## Composition

Prefer:
- wide horizontal compositions
- strong photography
- technical metadata
- asymmetric editorial rhythm
- disciplined whitespace
- restrained dividers
- full-width media where justified
- detail views that feel closer to premium industrial/product design than ecommerce

Avoid:
- generic three-card grids
- dashboard chrome
- ecommerce sale badges
- rounded SaaS card stacks
- gold accents used solely to imply wealth

## Photography direction

Primary:
- aircraft exterior
- air-to-air imagery
- hangars
- runway/tarmac
- cockpit or engineering details
- cabin materials when relevant

Secondary:
- destination context used sparingly
- operational detail

Avoid:
- champagne
- staged businessmen
- models walking onto jets
- generic "successful executive" stock photography

## Typography

Preferred characteristics:
- modern grotesk or neo-grotesk
- excellent numerals
- clear technical labels
- restrained use of uppercase metadata
- optional condensed companion for specifications

Do not reach for decorative serif simply because the industry is expensive.

## Palette

Good starting families:
- cold neutral + graphite
- white + aviation grey + restrained technical accent
- deep navy used as an operational color, not paired automatically with gold
- monochrome with one saturated accent

Avoid defaulting to:
- black + gold
- navy + gold
- beige + brass

## Information architecture

For a brokerage-oriented homepage:

1. Hero with precise market positioning
2. Selected aircraft / inventory preview
3. Brokerage or acquisition capability
4. Why the process is credible
5. Technical/service detail
6. Geographic or operational reach, only if factual
7. Advisor/contact path
8. Footer/legal

For a single-aircraft landing:

1. Aircraft identity and hero photography
2. Core specifications
3. Key differentiators
4. Cabin/interior
5. Performance and operating context
6. Gallery
7. Inquiry CTA
8. Legal/disclaimer

## Useful component vocabulary

- AircraftHero
- AircraftSpecificationStrip
- AircraftInventoryRow
- AircraftGallery
- AdvisoryServices
- AcquisitionProcess
- BrokerContact
- InquiryPanel
- TechnicalFacts
- GlobalCoverage

Names describe purpose, not visual style.

## Copy tone

Use:
- factual
- concise
- technically literate
- low-hype
- discreet

Prefer:
"2019 Gulfstream G500 available for acquisition"

over:
"Discover unmatched luxury in the skies"

## SEO considerations

Concept pages should still have realistic search intent.

Possible real-world intent families:
- aircraft for sale
- private jet broker
- aircraft acquisition advisory
- <model> for sale
- private aircraft sales <location>

Do not keyword-stuff fictional portfolio concepts.

## Trust signals

Use only when factual:
- transaction history
- certifications
- memberships
- OEM relationships
- years of experience
- offices
- aircraft under management

For fictional concepts, show the structural location where trust signals would appear without inventing externally verifiable claims.
